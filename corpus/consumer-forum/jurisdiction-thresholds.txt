# Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — Pecuniary Jurisdiction and Statutory Limitation Rules

## 1. Statutory Limitation Period
- Under Section 69 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, a complaint must be filed within TWO (2) YEARS from the date on which the cause of action has arisen.
- Cause of action arises on:
  - Date of deficiency in service or defect in goods.
  - Date of denial of refund/replacement by the seller/service provider.
  - Expiry of promised delivery/resolution date without fulfillment.

## 2. Pecuniary Jurisdiction Thresholds (as per Consumer Protection Rules)
- **District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (DCDRC):**
  - Jurisdiction: Value of goods or services paid as consideration does NOT exceed ₹50,00,000 (Rupees Fifty Lakhs).
  - Filing mode: District Commission in whose territorial jurisdiction the complainant resides, works, or where the cause of action arose.
- **State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (SCDRC):**
  - Jurisdiction: Value of goods or services paid exceeds ₹50,00,000 (Rupees Fifty Lakhs) but does NOT exceed ₹2,00,00,000 (Rupees Two Crores).
- **National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC):**
  - Jurisdiction: Value of goods or services paid exceeds ₹2,00,00,000 (Rupees Two Crores).

## 3. Mandatory Pre-Conditions
- Prior written communication (email, support ticket, or WhatsApp) demanding resolution from the seller/platform is highly recommended before filing.
- E-commerce platforms are designated as marketplace or inventory entities under E-Commerce Rules 2020 and are liable for deficiency in refund timelines.
